# ADE Human-Machine Framework

## ADE-IF Identity Framework Charter

**Status:** Foundational Draft
**Repository:** ADE-IF
**Version:** 0.1.0

---

## 1. Purpose

The ADE Identity Framework (ADE-IF) establishes a common semantic framework for representing identity-related concepts within the ADE Human-Machine Framework.

ADE-IF is intended to support consistent understanding of identities, identifiers, credentials, verification, authentication, authorization, delegation, trust relationships, and related identity structures across human, organizational, machine, and system environments.

ADE-IF builds upon the foundational concepts established by ADE-Core.

---

## 2. Vision

The vision of ADE-IF is to provide a common semantic foundation through which identities and identity-related relationships can be represented, understood, verified, and managed consistently across different jurisdictions, organizations, technologies, and implementations.

ADE-IF seeks to reduce ambiguity by distinguishing concepts that are frequently treated as interchangeable, including identity, identifier, authentication, verification, and authorization.

---

## 3. Mission

The mission of ADE-IF is to develop and maintain open standards that provide a consistent identity framework for human and machine understanding.

ADE-IF is intended to support interoperability between independent identity systems while allowing different implementations, technologies, authorities, and trust models to coexist.

---

## 4. Relationship to ADE-Core

ADE-IF extends ADE-Core.

ADE-IF uses the foundational ADE-Core concepts including:

* Entity
* Relationship
* Attribute
* State
* Time
* Location
* Intent

ADE-IF does not redefine ADE-Core concepts.

Instead, it establishes identity-specific concepts and relationships that build upon the ADE-Core foundation.

---

## 5. Scope

ADE-IF addresses identity-related concepts including:

* Identity
* Identifier
* Identity References
* Credentials
* Verification
* Authentication
* Authorization
* Delegation
* Trust Relationships
* Jurisdictional Identity
* Human and Non-Human Identity Representation

ADE-IF may evolve as additional requirements and use cases emerge.

---

## 6. Out of Scope

ADE-IF does not define:

* Government identity systems
* National identification programs
* Citizenship policy
* Immigration policy
* Authentication technologies
* Biometric standards
* Cryptographic standards
* Database architectures
* Specific credential technologies
* Specific identity providers

ADE-IF establishes semantic standards rather than implementation requirements.

---

## 7. Foundational Principles

### 7.1 Identity Is Not an Identifier

An identity and an identifier are distinct concepts.

A single identity may have multiple identifiers.

An identifier may be assigned by a specific authority, organization, or system.

---

### 7.2 Identity Is Not Authorization

Being identified does not imply permission.

Authorization should remain distinguishable from identity.

---

### 7.3 Authentication Is Not Identity

Authentication demonstrates control of an identity mechanism.

Authentication alone does not necessarily establish the underlying identity.

---

### 7.4 Verification Is Distinct from Authentication

Verification and authentication address different questions.

Authentication may demonstrate control.

Verification may establish whether a claim is true.

---

### 7.5 Jurisdictions May Maintain Independent Identity Authorities

Different jurisdictions, organizations, and systems may maintain their own identity authorities and identifiers.

ADE-IF should support interoperability without requiring a single global authority.

---

### 7.6 Information Should Remain with Authoritative Sources

Identity-related information should remain distinguishable from references to that information.

A framework should support authoritative sources without requiring unnecessary duplication of information.

---

### 7.7 Human and Non-Human Entities May Possess Identity

Identity concepts may apply to:

* Humans
* Organizations
* Devices
* Services
* Software Agents
* Artificial Intelligence Systems
* Vehicles
* Other identifiable entities

---

## 8. Interoperability

ADE-IF is intended to support interoperability between:

* Government identity systems
* Organizational identity systems
* Federated identity systems
* Self-sovereign identity systems
* Device identity systems
* Future identity models

ADE-IF does not require any single identity model to be adopted.

---

## 9. Standards Development

ADE-IF is intended to develop through the ADE standards-development process.

The current lifecycle is:

**Idea → Discussion → Proposal → Working Draft → Challenge → Review → Decision → Integration → Publication**

This lifecycle may evolve through ADE governance processes.

---

## 10. Governance

ADE-IF is governed through the ADE standards-development process.

Changes should be documented, reviewed, challenged where appropriate, and incorporated through established governance procedures.

---

## 11. Current Status

This document represents the initial charter of the ADE Identity Framework.

It is a **Foundational Draft** and is expected to evolve through technical review, community discussion, practical testing, and standards development.

---

## 12. Foundational Principle

> **Identity, identification, authentication, verification, and authorization are distinct concepts and should not be treated as interchangeable.**

ADE-IF seeks to establish a common semantic framework through which these concepts can be represented and understood consistently across human and machine environments.
